# Asset audit — what was uploaded, what it is worth, where it goes

87MB across `public/images/_inbox/`. **Read in full: three PDFs, four dropship case studies with
their written notes, four analytics sheets, seven store mockups, and about twenty product photos.**

**Verdict up front: this is not a folder of photos. It is a portfolio, and it is better than most of
what the twelve competitor sites are running.** It solves the single worst problem the site has.

---

## The finding that matters more than any image

Three of the uploaded assets carry this line:

> **"EcommerceNinjaa · 15 years in ecommerce, own Amazon + Shopify stores since 2011"**

And one carries: **"Shine & Sweat — my own Shopify store."**

Meanwhile the live site says *"We are new. We are not going to borrow someone else's results."*

**Both are true and the site is only telling one of them.** The client review's worst finding was
that the pages are *"confident claims with zero evidence… the whole thing could be one person"* —
anonymity plus confidence plus no price reading as the exact automation pattern the buyer fears.

**That is fixed by a sentence you already own:**

> **Hyprr Brands is new. The operator is not. Fifteen years in ecommerce, own Amazon and Shopify
> stores since 2011.**

It is not borrowing anyone's results. It is not a client claim. **It is the difference between a
new company and an inexperienced one, and the site currently reads as the second.**

**This is the highest-value change available and it costs one paragraph.**

---

## The brand-name problem, and why it is an opportunity

Every asset except the private-label PDF is branded **EcommerceNinjaa** — watermarks across the
analytics sheets, footer strips on the dropship slides and store mockups, cover pages on two PDFs.

**Do not strip the attribution.** An unattributed spreadsheet is a stock image; an attributed one is
evidence. The honest treatment, which is also the strongest:

> **A Hyprr-styled caption strip replacing the EcommerceNinjaa footer, reading:
> *"From the operator's own stores, trading as EcommerceNinjaa since 2011."*"**

That keeps the provenance, kills the brand collision, and turns the thing that looked like a
problem into the proof.

## Asset by asset

### 1 · `Hyprr_Product_Development_Launch_Case_Study_CLEAN.pdf` — **the best single asset you have**

18 pages, **already branded HYPRR BRANDS**, 16 sections, 10 embedded product renders.

Sections: executive summary · problem and customer insight · competitive landscape · design
lifecycle (10 stages, each with decision and outcome) · dimension evolution and engineering logic ·
folding mechanism · groove system · selected kitchen mat direction · selected faucet mat direction ·
**go / no-go decision log** · supplier and first-order economics · launch strategy · prototype and
validation gate · final product system · **risk, IP and claims discipline** · portfolio takeaway.

**Why it matters:** `/private-label` currently *asserts* that every product gets a written verdict
and never shows one. This is that verdict, at 18 pages, with the dimensions rejected and the reason
they were rejected. The client review said the pages *"sell judgement and never show a verdict."*
**This is the verdict.**

It is also honest in exactly the right way. It says of itself: *"the visuals are concept/AI-assisted
product renders used for industrial-design communication. Final manufacturing requires CAD,
tolerance stack-up, material testing, hinge-cycle testing, packaging validation, and IP clearance."*
**Ship that sentence with the renders.** A disclosed concept render is an asset; an undisclosed one
is a liability.

**Two layout bugs to fix before publishing:** the competitive-landscape table and the dimension
table both overflow their columns — text overlaps and runs past the right margin on pages 4 and 6.

**Placement:** `/documents` as the flagship download · `/private-label` hero and mid-page renders ·
`/about` as evidence of the operator · the source for a `/insights` article.

### 2 · Wholesale analytics — 4 masked sheets + a 5-page PDF · **the rarest asset in the category**

`sheet-01 profitability and sales analytics` is thirteen real products costed to the cent — COGS,
storage, fulfilment, ship and prep, buy box, referral fee, profit, ROI — **with an ORDER column
that is blank on the lines not worth buying.**

Competitor research measured this exact asset type as the rarest across twelve agency sites; only
one leads with real data. **You have it and they do not.** The blank ORDER cells are
*"we do not buy anything we cannot defend"* as evidence rather than a claim.

The PDF's own line is the right caption: *"The sheet I actually buy from. Anonymised here; the maths
is untouched."*

**Placement:** `/wholesale-ecommerce` hero and mid-page · `/documents` · the homepage proof band.

### 3 · Dropship product research — 4 products × 5 slides + written case notes · **publishable as-is**

Hand massager · deep-tissue massager · feminine balance gummy · gecko toy. Each: why this product ·
the product · demand trends · ad costs · supplier. Each with a written case study.

**The writing is genuinely good.** The gummy one argues *against* the two screens everyone checks
first and buys anyway on lifetime value: *"Judge this on the first sale and you kill your best

They are disciplined too — real cited anchors (CDC prevalence, market CAGR, Meta 2026 medians),

testimonials, named clients, or claimed client results."*

**Placement:** `/shopify-dtc` and `/ppc-paid-media` mid-page · **four ready-made `/insights`
articles** — which is the content layer that has been blocked on keyword measurement, now unblocked
because these are written · `/documents`.

### 4 · Shopify store mockups — 7 · **proof of DTC capability**

Three annotated cards (browser chrome, real product page, three labelled detail crops: problem /

**Weakest group and the one needing most editing.** The Amazon shots are on white and usable after

## The editing pass — what actually has to happen

Nothing ships raw. Six operations, in order of how many assets they touch:

**1 · Replace every EcommerceNinjaa footer and watermark** with the Hyprr caption strip above.
The analytics sheets carry a diagonal repeating watermark that needs removing at pixel level, not
cropping — it runs across the data.

**2 · Recolour to the Hyprr palette.** The dropship slides are teal, green and orange; teal maps
close to Aqua `#45D8C0`, the green to Lime `#B8F34A`, and the orange band should become Build
`#FFC84A`. Headers and accent rules only — **do not recolour the data.**

**3 · Background removal to transparent PNG** on every product shot that will float on a page:
the Amazon listing images, the massager, and the ten renders extracted from the PL PDF. This is what
makes a product read as placed rather than pasted, and it is the specific thing the reference image
does.

**4 · Crop tight.** The analytics sheets include spreadsheet chrome — row numbers, column letters,
the formula bar. Crop to the table. A screenshot that shows Excel reads as a screenshot; one that
shows only the table reads as a system.

**5 · Extract the 10 renders from the PL PDF** rather than screenshotting pages — they are embedded
at full resolution on pages 1, 3, 6, 7, 8 (×2), 9, 10 and 15 (×2).

**6 · Resize and compress.** Everything to 640 / 1280 / 1920 WebP, under 200KB at 1280, through the
pipeline the dev already built.

## The one thing to be careful about

The store mockups show a live store page carrying *"Rated 4.8/5.0 From 2,830+ Satisfied Customers"*
and *"87% Experience Noticeable Relief."*

**Those are that store's claims, not Hyprr's**, and showing a screenshot of a page you built is
evidence of the build. Caption it as such — *"a page we built for our own store"* — and it is
straightforward. Present it without that framing and it reads as Hyprr's result. **The caption is
the whole difference and it is one line.**
