> ### Why the H1 changed — the one positioning fix in this spec
> 
> This is not a repositioning. It is the page finally saying what the approved positioning already says. The hero currently promises "we run it"; the ownership section four screens down promises "you own it, you approve it, we operate it". Those are different offers, and roughly half of visitors never reach the correction.
> 
> The replacement resolves it in the first three seconds, carries "the operation" — the semantic centre of the keyword set in K — and survives the test that matters most here: someone who lost money to an automation firm reads "we run it" as the thing that happened to them, and "you own the business" as the opposite. The original line is not wasted; it opens the introduction in A.05, where it is an argument rather than a promise.
> 
> **If you want the original H1 kept**, it needs the ownership clause attached or the contradiction stands: *"We don't advise on ecommerce. We run the operation you own."* Weaker rhythm, same job. What cannot ship is the current line unmodified.

## H. PPC placement

_PPC appears in nine places, is a named service page, and is a headline service in none of them. That balance is deliberate._


> ### The reasoning, in one paragraph
> 
> A prospect must be able to see within one screenful that Hyprr runs paid acquisition — otherwise a private-label or Shopify buyer assumes they need a second agency, and you lose the engagement. But leading with PPC is the worst position available: the organic SERP is held by specialist incumbents with years of case studies, the buyers are single-service and price-comparing, and a PPC-only client gives you no control over inventory, pricing or listings — the three things that actually determine whether the advertising works. You would be accountable for a result you cannot influence. ## I. Managed / full-service messaging

_The capability is real and it sells. The vocabulary around it is the thing that has to be handled carefully._


> ### Why the page never says "done for you"
> 
> It fails on two counts. It is **the exact phrase** used by the ecommerce operators that have been shut down in this category, so a buyer who has been burned — your best-qualified prospect — is scanning for it specifically. And it is **inaccurate here**: your client approves every purchase, so it is not done *for* them. The whole family fails the same way: turnkey, hands-free, set-and-forget, we-do-everything, passive, fully automated.

## N. GEO and entity requirements

_A meaningful share of buyers will ask an AI system about this category before they open a results page. These are the conditions that decide whether Hyprr is in the answer._


> ### The one technical decision that matters most
> 
> **AI crawlers do not execute JavaScript.** Every indexable page must return complete HTML from the server — statically generated or server-rendered. The test is blunt: disable JavaScript and load the page. If it is blank, that is exactly what an AI retrieval system sees.
> 
> **This page has a specific exposure.** The four scroll states hold roughly a third of the site's service vocabulary and are driven by scroll position. All four must be in the served HTML whatever the scroll behaviour does with them visually. If they are injected by script, a third of what Hyprr does is invisible to every AI answer system — and to Google's text-only fallback.


**The seven facts the page must establish unambiguously**


| # | Fact | Where it is stated in plain language |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **What Hyprr is** | Hero H1 + supporting copy · FAQ Q1 · Organization `description`. All three say the same thing in the same words. |
| 2 | **Who operates it** | Team section with named people and live LinkedIn profiles · `/about` · Person schema once P.01 is answered. **This is the weakest link today** — an entity with no named humans is hard for an AI system to trust or attribute. |
| 3 | **What Hyprr does** | Three engines (12 named services) · scroll states · fully managed matrix (~40 named tasks). The matrix is the highest-value block on the page for retrieval, because a task is quotable and an adjective is not. |

**The banned-phrase gate — wire into CI as a failing check**


```
grep -rinE "guaranteed (profit|sales|roi|return|ranking)|passive income|risk-free|\
hands-free|turnkey|set and forget|done.for.you|we do everything|unlock|seamless|\
effortless|elevate|transform|holistic|end-to-end solution|supercharge|\
[0-9]+% (roi|return|growth|increase)" \
  --include="*.html" --include="*.tsx" --include="*.mdx" .
```


> ### The check will flag your best copy — a human reads the hits, nobody auto-fixes them
> 
> I ran this across every shipping line in section A. It returns exactly three hits, and all three are correct: **"No guaranteed returns"** in the transparency rows, and **"Expecting passive income"** and **"Expecting guaranteed returns"** in the not-a-fit column. Naming what you refuse is the argument — those lines are the point.
> 
> So the gate blocks the build and a person reviews the hits against that list of three. **Do not add a negation carve-out to the regex** — a pattern clever enough to pass "no guaranteed returns" will also pass "no client has failed to see guaranteed returns". A short approved-exceptions list checked by eye is safer than a smarter expression.
