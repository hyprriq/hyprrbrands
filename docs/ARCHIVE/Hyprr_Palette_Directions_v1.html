Palette directions

Four alternatives to a violet primary, each with a named reference, verified contrast against Hyprr’s existing accents, and an honest note on how guessable it is. Companion to the surface review — everything else in that document stands regardless of which of these you pick.

Short answer

You’re right, and for a better reason than the one you gave. Violet isn’t just common — it is structurally the wrong primary for the palette you already own.

It is the only colour in your system with no relatives. And when you try to use it as a large field, it is the only candidate that cannot carry your own accents at readable contrast. That is a measurable defect, not a matter of taste — the numbers are in section 0.

0Why violet is the wrong primary — two hard reasons

1. Violet is an orphan in your own palette

Plotted by hue, the Hyprr expressive palette clusters into two families — warm (Coral, Citrus) and cool-green (Lime, Aqua, Sky). Violet sits alone with nothing next to it:

Between Sky at 211° and Rose at 345° there is a 134° gap, and Violet is stranded in the middle of it. Every other colour has a neighbour. This is why the page feels like “something is missing” even when nothing is technically wrong — your primary and your accents are not from the same world, so the accents read as decoration rather than as a system.

There is a second tell hiding in the neutrals. Ink #17151F has a hue of 252° — it is a violet-tinted near-black. So violet isn’t just the accent; it is quietly in the greys too. Change the primary and the neutral should follow it, or the page will keep carrying a purple cast you can’t point at.

2. Violet cannot carry your own accents as a field

This is the part that settles it. Take each candidate field colour and measure your accents sitting on top of it:

Where this leaves us

My earlier recommendation of a deeper violet field was the right mechanic — commit a saturated ground instead of a 44px button — applied to the wrong hue. The mechanic still holds for every option below. Keep the argument, change the colour.

1The test each option has to pass

Five filters. Anything that fails two is out, however good it looks in isolation.

What I’d do

Direction A, with Petrol held in reserve. Here is the case: the three-engine colour coding is already in your components — ThreeEngines.tsx assigns Citrus to Build, Lime to Grow, Aqua to Operate, and SystemScroll.tsx carries the same mapping through all four states. The system is built. It is just being rendered at 7 pixels.

Making that the identity means colour on your site carries information instead of decoration — a visitor learns within two sections that amber means Build and teal means Operate, and every page after that is faster to read. No competitor in your category does this, because none of them has a three-engine model to code. It is the one palette decision that is downstream of your actual business rather than of a mood board.

And it is the cheapest to reverse. If in three months it feels too austere, adding Petrol as a field colour on top of a working engine-coded system is a two-token change. Going the other way is not.

3One note on the fonts, since the brand is open

You asked me to check the skills. There is one thing in there worth telling you even though you didn’t ask: Space Grotesk and Inter are both on impeccable’s reflex-reject font list — the training-data defaults that create monoculture. You are running both.

The same document says the reject list applies to new choices, and that when a brand has already committed, identity preservation wins. So this is not a recommendation to change them. It is a flag: if you are reopening the palette anyway, the fonts are the other half of “why does this look like every other site”, and changing both at once costs barely more than changing one.

If you want to look

Keep the grotesque register — it is right for the business. Look at Archivo (what this document is set in), Söhne, Founders Grotesk, Diatype, Neue Haas Grotesk, or Aeonik for display; pair with a single well-drawn text face rather than a second grotesque. And a proper mono for the operational labels — the build currently falls back to ui-monospace, which renders differently on every machine and is why the mono labels look inconsistent between your screenshots.

Do this second, not first. The palette decision changes what the page feels like; the font decision refines it. Doing both in the same week means you cannot tell which one worked.
