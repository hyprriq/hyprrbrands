# Visuals and video — the workstream, ordered by leverage

**Measured, 2 Sep: two on-page images across twenty-one routes, both incidental.** No photography,
no illustration, no diagram, no video. The site is type, colour and layout only. Share cards exist
but render off-site in a social preview — they do nothing for a visitor already on the page.

Owner is right that this is not a polish item. An agency site with nothing to look at reads as
unfinished, and every competitor on these SERPs has imagery.

---

## The unlock that changes what is possible here

**The no-faces rule was never a rule against pictures. It was a rule against *fake* pictures** —
no stock photography, no AI-generated faces, because one reverse image search ends a positioning
built on being checkable.

**Real footage of the real founder is the exact opposite of that.** It is the most costly-to-fake
signal available, and nothing about the existing constraint blocks it. That reframes the whole
problem: the site has not been short of *imagery*, it has been short of *proof*, and video is the
densest proof there is.

It also settles a blocker that has been open for weeks. `/about` needs "one real person". A
LinkedIn URL is a link to a profile anyone can make. **A founder talking to camera about how the
fee is calculated is a person who cannot be faked, and it is a better answer to the same
question.**

---

## Tier 1 · Video — highest leverage, and it needs the owner, not the dev

Two pieces. Neither needs a studio, a crew or a script read from a teleprompter — a phone on a
tripod, a window for light, and a quiet room beats a produced corporate video for this audience,
because the audience is specifically sceptical of polish.

**1 · Who runs this and why — 90 to 120 seconds. Goes on `/about` and the homepage.**
The founder paragraph already drafted in `docs/content/about-and-legal.md` is the script. Say it
in your own words rather than reading it. The single most valuable sentence is the specific thing
that went wrong somewhere and what you concluded from it — same note as the written version, and
it matters more on camera.

**2 · How the fee actually works — 2 to 3 minutes. Goes on `/how-we-work#fees`.**
Walk through the worked example out loud. This is the strongest video available to this business
and almost nobody in the category has made it: the measured `amazon agency pricing` SERP has nine
near-identical written posts and no operator explaining their own mechanic to camera. It doubles
as the YouTube and LinkedIn anchor asset the marketing plan already calls for.

**Both need:** captions burned or as a track (most views are muted), a real transcript on the page
for crawlers and AI answer engines, self-hosted or a privacy-mode embed so it does not drop
third-party cookies, and lazy loading with a poster frame so it costs nothing on LCP.

---

## Tier 2 · Diagrams — ship now, no owner input needed

Drawn from the existing design tokens. No photography, no stock, no delivery-date risk. **These
explain the business rather than decorate it, which is the brand brief's own test.**

Four, in priority order:

1. **How realised margin is calculated** — settlement, less tax, less cost of goods on units sold,
   less attributable advertising, plus the refund term. **This one is urgent for a second reason:
   PROMPT_12 removes the three fee tables, and this diagram is what should occupy the space they
   leave.** Same section, better content — the tables stated a price, the diagram proves a method.
2. **The approval gate** — who decides, what gets written down, what cannot proceed without a
   typed approval. The ownership argument as a picture.
3. **The operating cycle** — what happens weekly, monthly, quarterly. Turns "fully managed" from
   an adjective into a schedule.
4. **What is yours vs what Hyprr does** — accounts, capital, inventory, brand on one side; work on
   the other. Currently prose on four separate pages.

**Constraints:** brand tokens only · no decorative motion · legible at 375px · real text in the
SVG, not outlined paths, so it is selectable and readable by crawlers · works in the Petrol
sections without a raised surface, which the contrast measurement already showed is where the
tokens behave.

---

## Tier 3 · Real screenshots — the day there is something real to shoot

The portal, a purchase-order approval log, an actual weekly report with the numbers blurred. These
beat every illustration because they are evidence. Available the day there is a client, and the
blank reporting template is available now — which is also what unblocks `/documents`.

---

## Tier 4 · Editorial illustration — only if a real designer makes it

A consistent illustration system is genuinely good and genuinely hard. Half-committed
illustration is worse than none. Not a fill-in for tiers 1 and 2.

---

## Still banned, and this does not reopen

Stock photography · AI-generated faces · generic "team collaborating at a laptop" imagery ·
reserved aspect ratios for assets with no delivery date. The last one already cost the homepage
team section once.

## On "even for Google" — the accurate version

Worth being precise, because overclaiming here is the same error the fee copy was just cleaned of.

**Not true:** images and video are not direct ranking factors. Adding them does not move a
position by itself.

**True, and it is enough:**
- Video and image results are their own SERP surfaces with their own competition, and this
  category has almost no operator video in it.
- Video thumbnails in results raise click-through on the listing that already ranks.
- Engagement and dwell are what a page earns when there is something to look at, and a
  wall-of-text page on a considered purchase loses visitors who never scroll.
- AI answer engines increasingly surface video, and a transcript on the page is a large,
  well-structured, quotable passage of exactly the kind §27 and the GEO work already optimise for.
- Every competitor on these SERPs has imagery. Parity is not an advantage; its absence is a
  disadvantage.

**The strongest argument is none of those.** It is that this business is asking a sceptical buyer
to trust it with capital, and a page with no face, no voice and nothing to look at is asking that
in the least convincing form available.
