Homepage surface review

A read of the built homepage against Lemon Squeezy, Pattern and My Amazon Guy — and against Hyprr’s own brand guidelines, which the build is currently breaking in four places. Layout stays. Colour, type scale, density, motion and the scroll section change.

Source: D:\Projects\hyprrbrands 19 sections read Next 16.3 · Tailwind 4 No code modified
The diagnosis in one paragraph

The page isn’t suffering from too much padding. It is suffering from type that is too small and colour that never commits — and those two together are what read as emptiness.

You have a six-colour expressive palette and you are spending it on 7 px squares and 22 px number chips. Lemon Squeezy takes one purple and floods an entire viewport with it. That is the whole difference. Meanwhile the majority of the page’s text sits between 9.5 px and 15.5 px — your own brand guidelines set a 16 px web minimum — so a 1280 px column carries about 30% of a 1920 px screen in actual ink. Wide container, tiny content, no colour fields. That is the “clumsy and empty” feeling, and none of it requires a layout change to fix.

0Where the three references actually differ from you

You named Lemon Squeezy, Pattern and My Amazon Guy. They have almost nothing in common with each other visually — which is useful, because it isolates the three things they do share, and you are missing all three.

That is the “glowing, I want to log in again” feeling you described. It is not a gradient, not a glow effect, not an animation. It is a solid rectangle of committed colour.

The uncomfortable part

Your brand guidelines already say this, on page 9: “Keep roughly 60–70% of each page quiet. Introduce one bold colour moment per screenful.” The build has kept 97% quiet and delivered one bold moment in nineteen sections. And page 4 restricts Violet to “CTAs, links, interactive states, selected UI and small brand moments” — which the developer has honoured literally, and it is the single rule most responsible for the flatness. That rule needs amending, not the layout. Violet stays the interactive colour; a darker sibling becomes a field colour. Section 3 has the exact values.

1What is actually wrong

Seven findings, read from the source rather than from the screenshots. Each one names the file. Severities: P0 ships badly today · P1 materially hurts the page · P2 polish.

F1The type scale has 14 distinct sizes under 20px — and most of the page is below your own minimumP0

Across components/home/*.tsx the body-range sizes in use are:

9px · 9.5px · 10px · 10.5px · 11px · 11.5px · 12.5px · 13px · 13.5px · 14.5px · 15px · 15.5px · 16px · 16.5px · 19px
— 15 values where a scale should have four or five.

Frequency tells the story: text-[12.5px] appears 9 times, text-[10px] 8 times, text-[13.5px] 7 times, text-[10.5px] 7 times. Meanwhile text-base (16px) appears 9 times total across nineteen sections. The page is mostly set between 10 and 14 pixels.

Your brand guidelines, page 12, under NON-NEGOTIABLES: “Minimum body size: 16 px on web.” The build breaks that in roughly forty places. This is the “I can’t even see what it is” complaint, and it is not subjective — it is a spec violation.

FixCollapse to a six-step scale and delete every arbitrary value. 11px mono labels (uppercase only) · 15px meta · 17px body · 20px lead · 28/34px H3 · clamp(34px,4vw,60px) H2 · clamp(42px,5.4vw,76px) H1. Every card body and list item moves to 17px. Nothing that is a sentence is allowed below 16px.
F2The scroll section’s H2 is rendered at 11.5px grey uppercaseP0

In SystemScroll.tsx, the heading “How Hyprr builds and operates ecommerce businesses” — which your build spec calls “the most literal, most crawlable H2 on the page” — is styled:

<h2 style={{ fontSize: 11.5, letterSpacing: ".16em",
  textTransform: "uppercase", color: "#8e89a0" }}>

Eleven and a half pixels, uppercase, letter-spaced, in mid-grey, on a dark aubergine field. It is the section’s title and it reads as a system label. In the screenshot you sent, it is genuinely illegible.

FixPromote it to a real heading: clamp(28px,3.2vw,44px), weight 700, sentence case, in #FFFFFF, sitting above the state content rather than in the utility bar. The “STATE 01 / BUILD” eyebrow can stay small — an H2 cannot.
F3The scroll effect never resolves into a state — it is permanently mid-transitionP0

SystemScroll.tsx pins for SCROLL_LEN = 320 vh and drives every property — colours, grid tracks, opacities — through a continuous lerp against raw scroll progress, with a smoothstep ease. Four states across 320vh means each state owns ~80vh of scroll, and because the interpolation is continuous there is no dwell: the moment a state arrives it starts leaving.

That is exactly what “the effect is not smooth” describes. It is not a frame-rate problem. It is that the user never gets a still frame to read, so four states of content register as one long smear. Lemon Squeezy’s equivalent moments hold completely still and let you read them.

FixAdd dwell. Give each state a hold zone of ~55% of its scroll segment where progress is clamped and only the remaining 45% animates the handoff. Same 320vh, same code shape — one remap function on p before it reaches paint(). Section 5 has the curve.
F4On mobile, the entire visual half of the signature section is display:noneP0

Below 900px, applyMode() runs:

if (grid) grid.style.display = "none";
if (rail) rail.style.display = "none";
if (cue)  cue.style.display  = "none";

The four panels, the progress rail and the scroll cue are all removed. What remains is four stacked text blocks separated by hairlines. In Hero.tsx, the same pattern: the marketplace-listing and storefront-admin fragments are hidden min-[900px]:block, so they never appear on a phone either.

This is why the mobile experience reads as “a big story or documentary with no effects.” It literally is one. On a phone the homepage is nineteen sections of text with two surviving illustrations. Given that a majority of your traffic will be mobile, this is the most expensive finding in the document.

FixMobile gets its own composition, not a subtraction. The four states become a horizontally snapping carousel (scroll-snap-type: x mandatory, one panel per viewport, dot indicators) — no JS scroll-jacking, native momentum, works with a flick. The hero fragments stack as two real cards under the headline instead of disappearing. Detail in section 7.

F6The “wasted padding” is a measure problem, not a padding problemP1

Section padding is actually restrained — py-[clamp(64px,7vw,112px)] is normal for this genre and Lemon Squeezy uses more. The container is max-w-[1280px], also normal.

What is not normal is what happens inside it. In ThreeEngines.tsx the intro paragraph is capped at max-w-[62ch] at 16–18px. In Hero.tsx the supporting copy is capped at max-w-[540px]. So on a 1920px monitor: 1280px container, ~565px of actual text, and 1355px of nothing. The eye reads that as a page that failed to fill itself.

Your screenshot 2 is the proof — “Three engines. One operating partner.” runs 1000px wide, then the paragraph beneath it stops at 640px and leaves half the row blank. The heading and the body are on two different grids.

FixThree moves, no layout change. (a) Raise body type so 62ch occupies more width — 17px minimum, 20px for section ledes. (b) Give every section intro a right-hand occupant: a stat, a two-line pull-quote, a channel list, or the section’s CTA — so the row is two columns, not one column and a void. (c) Tighten the container to 1180px so the remaining margin reads as deliberate rather than as leftover.
F7Ambient drift on the hero fragments, and one identical fade-up everywhere elseP2

Five hy-drift animations run permanently in Hero.tsx, floating cards up and down forever at 55% and 50% opacity. Meanwhile Reveal.tsx applies the same translateY(18px) + fade to nine wrappers. That is the pattern your own build spec flags: “Ambient drift on hero fragments is the most common way a serious page starts to feel like a template — one deliberate movement beats five ambient ones.”

The half-transparent fragments are the bigger issue. At opacity-55 they read as unfinished rather than as depth, and they are competing with the H1 for attention while saying nothing.

FixDelete hy-drift and hy-drift-b entirely. Raise the fragments to full opacity and let z-order and shadow do the layering. Replace the uniform Reveal fade with staggered children inside a section rather than one block-level fade of the whole section. Section 6 lists what replaces it.

2The white background question

You proposed switching to full white with black text. You are two-thirds right, and the third that’s wrong matters.

Right: #FAFAF7 should stop being the page ground

Cloud #FAFAF7 against White #FFFFFF is a 1.05:1 difference. It is invisible. So today every white card sits on a ground that looks identical to it, and the cards only exist because of a 1px #E8E6EA border. That is why the page feels like a wireframe: the only thing separating content from background is a hairline. Screenshot 1 versus screenshot 4 in your set shows it — the whiter one does look better, and that is why.

So yes: make #FFFFFF the default page ground. Cards on white then need no border at all — a shadow or a tint does the job, and the page gains depth it currently has none of.

Wrong: black text, and white everywhere

Two corrections.

  • Not black — Ink #17151F. Your guidelines say it explicitly: “Avoid pure black; Ink is softer, more premium.” Ink on white is 18.05:1. Pure black adds nothing but harshness.
  • White everywhere is the same mistake you have now, in a brighter key. Nineteen white sections is as flat as nineteen cream ones. What Lemon Squeezy actually does is white plus hard colour bands. Remove the white-on-cream mush and replace it with a real alternation.
What Cloud is still good for

Don’t delete #FAFAF7 — repurpose it. It becomes the quiet band in the alternation (the section after a colour field, where the eye rests), and it stops being the default. Same token, different job, and it keeps the warmth the brand asked for without flattening the whole page. Where you want a slightly more visible quiet band, #F1EFF6 — a neutral tinted 0.01 toward violet — separates from white at 1.14:1 and reads as deliberate.

5Scroll-stop rebuild

Keeping the pinned mechanic, as agreed. Six changes to SystemScroll.tsx, none of which touch the layout or the state content.

#ChangeSpecification
1Add dwellRemap scroll progress before it reaches paint(). Per state segment: the first 20% and last 25% animate the transition; the middle 55% is clamped to the state value. Implementation is one function — const held = clamp01((seg - 0.20) / 0.55) applied per segment rather than a global smoothstep. The section then reads as four still frames with movement between them, which is what the Lemon Squeezy pages you liked actually do.
2Field colourSection background #25172F → #4E2FD6. Active panel keeps its pastel fill (citrus / lime / aqua / white). Inactive panels become rgba(255,255,255,.08) with a rgba(255,255,255,.16) border — translucent white on violet, not tinted ink. Removes the mud entirely.
3TypeH2 to clamp(28px,3.2vw,44px) white, sentence case (F2). State heading from clamp(24px,4vh,44px) to clamp(30px,3.4vw,52px) — switch the unit from vh to vw; the current vh basis is why it collapses to 24px on a 13″ laptop. Chip list from clamp(12.5px,1.7vh,15px) to a flat 17px.
4Panel contentToday the four panels are near-empty boxes with a caption. Each should carry one legible artefact, sized to be readable at a glance: 01 a three-row research shortlist · 02 a listing with a bid/search-term row · 03 a purchase order with the “Approved by client” line · 04 the four panels resolving into the loop. Panel 03 is the argument of the entire business — it should be the most legible thing in the section.
5Rail becomes a controlThe 01 BUILD / 02 GROW / 03 OPERATE / 04 SYSTEM rail is currently decorative. Make each tick a button that scrolls to that state’s hold zone. Costs nothing, converts a passive 320vh into something a skimmer can operate, and gives keyboard users a route through the section.
6Exit affordanceAdd a persistent “Skip to commerce paths” link in the pinned pane. A 320vh pin with no exit is the most common reason a scroll section is described as clumsy — people feel trapped rather than guided.
One thing to verify before shipping any of this

Your build spec (section N) requires all four states to be in the served HTML with JavaScript disabled, because AI crawlers don’t execute JS and this section holds roughly a third of the site’s service vocabulary. The current component renders all four in JSX and only styles them imperatively, so it should pass — but confirm it with JS off before touching the paint loop, and confirm again after. It is the easiest thing to break while adding dwell.

6Motion and interaction — without changing the layout

You asked what effects can be added. The answer starts with a subtraction: the page currently has five permanent ambient animations and one repeated fade, which is the exact profile that reads as generated. Delete those, then add six things that mean something.

Delete

  • hy-drift / hy-drift-b — five permanent floats in the hero
  • opacity-55 / opacity-50 on the hero fragments
  • The uniform Reveal fade applied to whole sections
  • hy-blink on the scroll cue — a blinking arrow is a 2011 device

Keep

  • The pinned scroll sequence (rebuilt per section 5)
  • hy-flow on the replenishment line in Commerce Paths — it explains a process
  • Reduced-motion kill switch in globals.css — already correct

Add — in priority order

#InteractionWhere and why
01Staggered children, not faded blocksReplace section-level Reveal with a per-child stagger: cards and list items enter at 40ms intervals, 12px rise, 420ms cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1). Same amount of code, ten times more alive, and it makes a three-card row read as a sequence rather than a slab.
02Channel row hoverSix marks sit inert. On hover, the mark goes from Ink to full colour and its micro-label slides up 2px. Instantly signals “these are real capabilities, not a logo wall” — which is exactly what the build spec says that row must communicate.
03The “You” node pulseYour build spec calls the DECISION → You node “the best piece of visual argument on the page.” Give it one violet ring pulse on hero entry, once, then stop. hy-decide is already defined in globals.css and appears to be unused — this is what it was written for.
04Engine card colour floodOn hover, each of the three engine cards fills with its accent tint (citrus / lime / aqua at ~12%) and its border takes the full accent. 180ms. Turns the flattest row on the page into the most tactile one, and reinforces the colour coding you already decided.
05Cost-stack drawThe data-cost bars already stagger via Reveal. Slow them: 700ms each, 120ms apart, with the “Build / service” segment drawing last so the visitor watches your own fee turn out to be the small one. Same code, better argument.
06FAQ accordion easing<details> currently snaps. Animate with interpolate-size: allow-keywords + grid-template-rows transition — no JS, no layout change, and it removes the cheapest-feeling interaction on the page.

What not to add: parallax, animated counters (your spec bans them — a counting number is an implied earnings claim), section-wide fade-ups, cursor followers, WebGL, autoplay video. Every one would push the page toward the template feeling you are trying to get away from.

8Imagery — and a disagreement about stock

You asked for stock images to make the page more interactive. You need imagery. You do not need stock photography, and buying the obvious kind would be the most damaging single decision available on this page.

Why I’m pushing back

Your own build spec, section F, lists four visual rules and the second is: “No stock photography of people, and no AI-generated faces. Not on the team section, not as ambient imagery. This is the one irreversible mistake available on this page.”

That rule is correct and it is worth defending. You are selling to people who have been burned by ecommerce “automation” operators, and those operators’ sites are wallpapered with smiling stock consultants and handshake photos. A stock businessman on hyprrbrands.com puts you in that visual category on sight, and it cannot be undone once a sceptical buyer has made the association. My Amazon Guy gets away with a person in the hero because it is Steven Pope’s actual face, and he is the proof. That is founder photography, not stock.

What the page actually needs

Pattern is the useful reference here, not Lemon Squeezy — it alternates CSS UI with real photography, and that is why it never feels like a template. Four categories, in order of value:

#AssetBrief
01Founder / team photography
Commission		The highest-value image on the site and the one blocker your build spec already flags. One real photograph of one real person, consistent crop, plain background, natural light. A phone in good light beats a studio shoot you keep postponing. Until it exists, ship typographic cards — initials on a colour field — never a silhouette.
02Operational still life
Shoot or licence — objects only		This is the “stock” you can safely use, because it contains no people and makes no claim. Cartons on a pallet, a barcode label being applied, poly mailers, a tape gun, a prep bench, a stack of cases under warehouse light. Shot tight and warm, used as full-bleed background behind the Ownership or True-cost sections at 20–30% overlay. It says “physical goods, real operation” — which is precisely the thing that separates you from a marketing agency.
03Product / packaging renders
Create		For the Private Label path card. An unbranded mailer box and a simple carton, photographed or rendered in Hyprr colours. Must not imply a real client brand — spec requirement.
04Elevated UI artefacts
Rebuild what exists		You already have these — they are just small, translucent and drifting. Make them the imagery: full opacity, larger, one per section, on a real shadow. A purchase order with an approval line, a replenishment flow, a margin-by-SKU row. These are more distinctive than any photograph you could licence, because nobody else in the category shows their actual work product.

If you do licence stock

Search the physical object, never the category. “Cardboard cartons stacked on a wooden pallet under warehouse strip lighting” returns something usable; “ecommerce business” returns the exact library your competitors used. Unsplash is fine for category 02 and free for commercial use. Two rules: no faces, and no screens showing numbers — a laptop displaying a rising graph is an implied earnings claim and your spec bans those in visuals as firmly as in copy.

The cheapest credibility asset you are not using

Not photography — the six channel marks. Amazon, Walmart, eBay, TikTok Shop, Shopify, Target already sit in your Channels row as inert grey glyphs. Given colour on hover, correct optical sizing and a micro-label each, that row does more for trust in one screenful than any photograph, and it costs a day. Confirm usage rights per marketplace first — spec item P.05.
