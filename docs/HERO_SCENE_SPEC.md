# Hero scene spec — the isometric operating scene

Owner reference: the Ecomersify hero — a 3D isometric scene with a monitor at the centre, floating
UI cards, a shopping bag, an Amazon carton, a delivery truck and a warehouse, connected by glowing
paths on a pale ground.

**The format is right and this spec adopts it.** One image that explains the whole operation is
worth more than five diagrams, and it is the "connected stack" — channels in, desk in the middle,
fulfilment out — expressed as a picture rather than a list.

---

## The one thing that cannot be copied

**Every number in that reference is a fabricated result:** ₹24,56,00,000 total revenue · +28.6% ·
4.6x ROAS · +32.1% · 34.6% ROAS · AOV ₹1,205 +8.6% · 18,785 repeat customers +15.3% · 4.8 stars from
2,400 reviews.

On Hyprr's site those are **earnings claims and fabricated social proof.** The site publishes
`/earnings-claims`, which states that no income figures, no projected returns and no results
promises appear anywhere. A hero image full of invented revenue would contradict the one policy page
the whole positioning rests on — and it would do it in the most visible asset on the site.

**It is also the tell.** The measured `is amazon automation a scam` SERP is full of exactly this
imagery: dashboards showing revenue that belongs to nobody. The sceptical buyer arriving from that
SERP reads a fabricated revenue chart as confirmation, not as proof.

> ### The rule for every scene
> **The cards show states and actions, never results.**
> *"PO approved by client"* not *"+28.6% revenue"*. *"Cover: 6 weeks"* not *"₹24,56,00,000"*.
> This is a constraint that makes the asset better: **every competitor's hero shows numbers that are
> not theirs. Hyprr's shows the work, which is the thing being sold.**

## Five scenes, one per archetype

Matches `PROMPT_18`. Each is the hero visual for its archetype and replaces the at-a-glance panel as
the primary focal point (the panel moves beneath it).

### 1 · Trading loop — `/wholesale-ecommerce`
**Centre:** a purchase-order document on screen, mid-approval.
**Left, flowing in:** supplier cartons, a brand catalogue, a price tag.
**Right, flowing out:** Amazon and Walmart cartons, a delivery truck, a warehouse rack.
**The loop is the point** — a visible path from warehouse back to the PO, because the money cycles.
**Cards:** `Supplier verified` · `Category ungated` · `Margin floor met` · `PO awaiting your approval`
· `Cover 6 weeks` · `Reorder point reached`
**One card must read `Do not buy`** — the refusal is the differentiator and it belongs in the hero.

### 2 · Gated project — `/private-label`
**Centre:** a verdict sheet with eight axes, one marked red.
**Left:** a product concept, a sample box, a packaging mock.
**Right:** a finished branded unit on an Amazon carton.
**Between them:** five visible gates, one closed.
**Cards:** `Trademark filed` · `Testing complete` · `Importer recorded` · `Sample approved` ·
`Verdict: reject — review moat` · `Gate open: compliance`
The closed gate is the composition's focal tension. **Most candidates die here and the picture
should say so.**

### 3 · Build then run — `/shopify-dtc`, `/ecommerce-website-development`
**Centre:** a storefront on a monitor, exploded into layers — offer, pricing, storefront, checkout,
acquisition, retention.
**Two paths leaving the scene:** one to a handover document, one to an operating desk.
**Cards:** `Checkout configured` · `Repository yours` · `Domain in your name` · `Handover, or we
keep running it`

### 4 · Cadence desk — `/ecommerce-operations`, `/marketplace-management`, `/shopify-management`
**Centre:** a calendar grid — daily, weekly, monthly — not a chart.
**Around it:** a case queue, a listing sweep, an inventory shelf, a report.
**Cards:** `Inventory checked — daily` · `Cases worked — daily` · `Listings swept — weekly` ·
`Policy change found` · `Report issued`
**No revenue anywhere.** This scene's argument is that the work happens whether or not anything is
wrong.

### 5 · Constraint and lever — `/ecommerce-growth`, `/marketplace-growth`, `/ppc-paid-media`
**Centre:** five gates in a row — demand, conversion, inventory, margin, capacity — **one visibly
narrower than the rest.**
**Levers beside it**, one pulled.
**Cards:** `Constraint: fulfilment` · `Buy box won` · `Bid capped by cover` · `Recommendation: wait`
**`Recommendation: wait` is the most important card in the set** and it is the one no competitor
would ever put in a hero.

## Look and construction

**Palette — this is where Hyprr separates from the reference.** The Ecomersify scene is violet on
lavender, which is the default for this style and is why every site using it looks alike.

Hyprr uses **Petrol `#0A4E5C` as the ground** with the engine colours as the accents — Build
`#FFC84A`, Grow `#B8F34A`, Operate `#45D8C0` — and each scene leads with **its own engine colour**.
That alone makes five scenes that read as one family and as nobody else's.

**Construction rules:**
- Isometric, single light source, soft contact shadows. **No gradients on objects** — flat surfaces
  with one shade, per the brand guidelines' warning against excessive gradients.
- Cards are the site's own card style — white, `--color-line` border, radius 28, the real typeface.
  They should look lifted from the product, not from a stock kit.
- Real platform logos only where a logo is a fact: Amazon, Walmart, Shopify. **No invented brands and
  no client logos.**
- **No people, no faces, no hands.**
- Delivered as **SVG where possible, otherwise WebP at 2x** with a transparent or Petrol ground.
- `alt` text describes the operation, not the picture: *"The wholesale buying loop — supplier
  verified, category ungated, purchase order awaiting client approval, stock inbound to Amazon and
  Walmart"*. That alt text is also a passage an AI answer engine can quote.

## How to actually get them made

| Route | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Commission an illustrator** (Blender or Spline) | Highest | The right answer for production. Five scenes as one brief with a shared kit — objects, cards and lighting reused — is far cheaper than five one-offs. |
| **Spline** — browser-based 3D | Low | A designer or the owner can build these directly. Exports to image or interactive. Realistic for a first version. |
| **AI generation for concepts only** | Low | Useful to hand a look to an illustrator. **Not for production** — the brand guidelines rule out work that reads as obviously AI-generated, and this style is the most recognisable AI look there is. |

**Sequence:** one scene first — the wholesale loop — and put it live. Judge it on the page before
commissioning the other four. **A five-scene brief with one approved scene in it is a much better
brief than five scenes commissioned blind.**

## What this replaces and what it does not

**Replaces:** the at-a-glance panel as the hero focal point. The panel stays, beneath the scene.

**Does not replace:** the mechanism diagrams, the rule card, the artefact or the phase timeline.
Those are mid-page and they do a different job — the hero scene sells, the diagrams explain.

**Asset count per service page after this:** hero scene · mechanism diagram · artefact · rule card ·
phase timeline · at-a-glance panel = **six**, against the owner's 5–6 target and the competitive
range of 22–80.

**Still true:** the rarest assets in the category are real screenshots and real charts, and Hyprr can
produce both. **The scene is the hero; the screenshot is the proof.** A site with a beautiful hero
and no evidence is still the automation pattern.
