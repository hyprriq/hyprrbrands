# Client review — the first-time seller with capital

The owner's instinct was right: **the pages are written for someone who already sells.** This is a
read of the live `/private-label` and `/wholesale-ecommerce` in the persona of the buyer who
actually has the money — 38, runs an unrelated business, $50k to invest, no ecommerce vocabulary,
suspicious of Amazon-automation ads, four minutes before the tab closes.

Everything below is quoted from the live pages.

---

## Finding 1 — the biggest one. Two doors, no sign on either.

**The persona could not tell which service was for them, and neither page tries to help.**

There is no sentence anywhere of the form *"wholesale is X, private label is Y, choose wholesale
if…"*. The private-label page never mentions wholesale in a comparing sentence. The wholesale page's
closest thing to a fit test — *"You have capital to deploy and want it working in a catalogue, not a
course"* — never names the alternative.

Worse: **the two pages are structurally identical.** Same eight headings, same "first 90 days", same
"what stays yours", same 30%. That makes them read as the same product twice and the choice feel
arbitrary.

> *"What I pieced together myself, from the homepage of all places: wholesale = buy other people's
> existing products and resell them; private label = make and brand your own. That took me three
> pages and I'm not confident."*

**This is the single biggest reason the tab closes.** The structural consistency we built as a
strength is, to an undecided reader, a liability.

---

## Finding 2 — jargon, and the one that matters most

**Wholesale page — undefined on first use:** catalogue line · replenishment · **buy box** · prep ·
sell-through · stranded and aged stock · account health · resale certificate · distributor vs brand.
Landed cost *is* defined — 800 words after first use.

**"Buy box" is the worst case.** It appears as *"the buy box decides whether you sell at that price
at all"* and again in *"reprice against the buy box"* — a thing that decides whether you sell at
all, never explained.

> *"A sentence that says a thing decides whether I sell at all, and doesn't say what it is, is where
> I start feeling stupid."*

**Private label — undefined:** private label itself (never defined on the page) · validation ·
**review moat** — used as the flagship example of judgement · DTC · patent-dense · and the five
compliance gates, which get five words each. *"Importer · responsible party"* is the one that could
carry personal liability and it gets three words.

**Credit where due:** FBA, ASIN, ACoS, A+ content and Seller Central never appear. The ungating FAQ
— *"Two separate gates that are often confused"* — is the best teaching on the site, and it is
buried 1,500 words down.

---

## Finding 3 — no price is now the loudest scam signal on the page

This is the cost of removing the figures, and it should be recorded plainly rather than argued
about.

> *"Everything else is unusually specific, and the one number I need is a formula. That asymmetry
> reads as deliberate."*

The six questions the persona would have to email to ask: what does it cost · is $50k enough ·
how much of it is fee vs stock vs ads · when does money come back · what does 30% actually cost in
dollars · **is the build fee refundable if the research says don't buy** — which matters most on
private label, where the page says most products are rejected.

**The fix is not reinstating the price list.** It is a **worked example with real arithmetic** —
capital deployed, a fee range, what 30% came to, what the client kept — plus a stated minimum:
*"below $X deployed this does not work and we will say so."* That satisfies the reader without
publishing a rate card, and it is consistent with the owner's decision.

**Also, free goodwill being thrown away:** `/how-we-work` publishes the step-down to 25% then 20%.
**Neither service page mentions it.**

---

## Finding 4 — confident claims, zero evidence

No client name, no case study, no result, no person, no years-in-business, no volume figure.

> *"Confident claims with zero evidence behind them is precisely the automation-ad pattern, even
> when the claims are modest. The whole thing could be one person."*

**Anonymity + confidence + no price is indistinguishable from the thing this reader already
distrusts** — and the copy is far too good to lose that way. Either put a named person with a face
and a background above the fold, or **say the quiet part out loud**: *"Hyprr is early. Here is who
runs it, here is what we have done before, and here is why we publish our fee mechanic instead of
testimonials."*

---

## Finding 5 — the only next step is a sales call

Both CTAs are *"Let's talk."* Nothing lower-commitment exists.

The pages sell **judgement** — *"Every product gets a written verdict"* — and never show one.

> *"There is no lower-commitment step — no guide, no example verdict, no calculator, nothing that
> lets me test them before I'm on the phone with a closer."*

A redacted sample verdict sheet or a sample landed-cost model would do more for trust than any
paragraph, and would filter tyre-kickers before the call.

---

## Finding 6 — the entity question, spotted unprompted

> *"© 2026 Hyprr Retail LLC with a US phone number, and the pages sell UK marketplaces. Which
> country am I contracting with, and who do I sue?"*

A US entity serving UK and Gulf clients is fine — but it has to be said, not left to be inferred
from a footer.

---

## What is working, and must not be touched

- *"The seller account, the inventory and the capital stay in your name throughout."* — **the single
  best sentence on either page**, and the reason the tab stays open.
- *"You can revoke them from your own account settings without involving us. We do not hold your
  login credentials."* — concrete and checkable.
- *"A provider who marks up your inventory has a reason to want more of it."*
- *"We do not promise that every line sells, and any provider who does is describing something other
  than wholesale."*
- *"If you find yourself receiving only approvals, that is a signal about the research, not about
  your luck."*
- Naming what is refused — *"Ingestibles and anything applied to skin…"*. **People who want your
  money don't usually list what they won't take.**

---

## Five-question scorecard

| | Private label | Wholesale |
|---|---|---|
| What they do | Partly — verb clear, noun undefined | **Yes** |
| Why trust them | **No** — governance language, zero evidence | **No** |
| What's different | Partly | Partly |
| What outcome | **No** — not one number, range or example | **No** |
| Why now | **No** | **No** |

**Three of five are a flat no on both pages.** The honest answer replaced the answer: *"we do not
promise that every line sells"* is true and admirable, and it left the outcome question blank.
